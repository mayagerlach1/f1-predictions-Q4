      How It Works

      Step 1: score every driver. Each factor is calculated from real data and scaled so the best driver scores 100.

      Season results

      Points per race started, including sprints. Rewards consistency across the year.

      Car pace

      Every finish by both team cars in the last 8 races. A great car lifts both drivers.

      Recent form

      The driver's last 6 races, weighted 6-5-4-3-2-1. Retirements are handled by retire risk instead.

      Qualifying speed

      Share of 2026 qualifying sessions where the driver reached Q3.

      Track history

      Finishes at this track over the last 3 years (50/30/20%). A retirement counts as 20th.

      Retire risk

      Real 2026 retirement rate, blended with the grid average so one bad weekend doesn't dominate.

      Wet skill v2

      Team-mate head-to-head in 4 genuinely wet races (Brazil 2024, Australia 2025, Britain 2025, Sepang 2026). Comparing team-mates removes the car's effect. Pulled toward 50% because the sample is tiny.

      Step 2: combine them. The sliders set each factor's weight. The defaults came from testing on rounds 7–18 and keeping what predicted real winners best. They were not re-tuned after Mexico, so future races stay an honest test.

      Step 3: add qualifying. Pole-sitters won 12 of the 17 races with a confirmed pole, so the pole-sitter gets a large boost once you enter it (smaller boosts for P2 and P3).

      Step 4: add weather. Half the simulations are run dry and half wet, then blended using your rain chance. In a wet race the model assumes more randomness (×1.4), more retirements (×1.5), and shifts each driver by their wet skill. These rain settings are assumptions: with only one wet race in 2026, there isn't enough data to tune them.

      Step 5: simulate. 40,000 races, each with random retirements and "race luck." A driver's win chance is the share of races they won.

      Limits. No practice pace, upgrades, tyre strategy or safety-car modelling. One race is very random. Treat these as well-informed odds, not a guarantee.
