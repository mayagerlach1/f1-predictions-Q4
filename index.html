      How It Works

      Step 1: score every driver. Each factor is calculated from real data and scaled so the best driver scores 100.

      Season results

      Points per race started, including sprints. Rewards consistency across the year.

      Car pace

      Every finish by both team cars in the last 8 races. A great car lifts both drivers.

      Recent form

      The driver's last 6 races, weighted 6-5-4-3-2-1. Retirements are handled by retire risk instead.

      Qualifying speed

      Share of 2026 qualifying sessions where the driver reached Q3.

      Track history

      Finishes at this track over the last 3 years (50/30/20%). A retirement counts as 20th.

      Retire risk

      Real 2026 retirement rate, blended with the grid average so one bad weekend doesn't dominate.

      Wet skill

      Team-mate head-to-head in 5 wet races (Brazil 2024, Australia 2025, Britain 2025, Sepang 2026, São Paulo 2026). Pulled toward 50% because the sample is small.

      Starting grid v3

      A log curve, chosen by testing: P1 to P2 is a big step, P15 to P16 a small one. Fitted on 9 races with official grids.

      Step 2: combine them. The sliders set each factor's weight. The defaults came from testing on rounds 7–18, and weren't changed after Mexico or São Paulo, so the final races stay an honest test.

      Step 3: add the starting grid. Once qualifying is done, each driver's grid slot is added using the log curve. If only the top 3 are entered, everyone else gets the average effect of a P4–P22 start.

      Step 4: set the luck. Each simulated race adds random "race luck." How much was calibrated on real races: before the grid is known, the model uses one level; with the grid it uses another, because the grid already explains a lot. The Chaos slider scales both (50 = calibrated).

      Step 5: weather. Half the simulations run dry and half wet, blended by your rain chance. In the wet: more luck (×1.4), more retirements (×1.5), and each driver shifts by their wet skill. These rain settings are assumptions; there are too few wet races to tune them.

      Step 6: simulate. 40,000 races. A driver's win chance is the share of races they won.

      Limits. No practice pace, upgrades, tyre strategy or safety-car modelling. Grids exist for only 12 of 20 rounds. One race is very random: treat these as well-informed odds, not a guarantee.
